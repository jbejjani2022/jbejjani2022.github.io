> Quacks and Wacks
> walking (hobbling) with gusto (blisters)
> in search of what's numinous
> in them

*Sunday, 22 March 2026.*
*A talk between two peregrinos.*

"People are so nice. And the pilgrim culture is cool."

"Yeah, it really is. It's such an interesting subculture in the world. The fact it exists and just will always exist is somehow strangely comforting to me. Buncha wounded quacks and wacks walking a lot."

"Yeah like what's going on in them to wanna do that? To walk like that? Why do hundreds of thousands from around the world do this every year for a thousand plus years? When I got to the pilgrim office, already 450 people had received the Compostela that day. Even if you aren't religious, it's interesting whatever's drawing you here. And you feel interconnected in some way. But then at times it felt so absurd."

"I get the interconnectedness... what do you mean absurd?"

"Like we're on this pilgrimage that started in 900 CE to follow the remains of a supposed saint, racking dozens of kilometers a day, hobbling into hostels feet like tenderized meat, sleeping beside above and below strangers who walked all day too and say 'buen camino,' following little signs of yellow shells, a scallop's home-no-more dangling from our packs, to reach a place where they built a very beautiful building for these remains of a person... It's like everyone has bought into a story and decided it's real and that's what gives it meaning and makes it something big, something you wouldn't want to change. But then you're like, 'Wait, it feels a bit arbitrary,' and then you're like, 'Well, why am I doing it anyway?' And you realize it's cause stories are fun and so are journeys that hurt and the remembering of what's inside you. And so, you want to *keep* walking."

"It's quite a bit like life."

"Does anyone really want to get to the cathedral?"

"They're all around, the cathedrals. Haven't I taught you anything?"
